## Exact convention

1. **Separate tours.** Analyze ATP and WTA separately. A batch key is exactly `(tour, source tourney_date)`. Preserve the original source label and event identity alongside it; do not turn it into an actual timestamp. Validate label syntax/calendar consistency solely to compare labels. Missing, invalid or ambiguous labels block the affected mapping with reasons; do not infer a replacement from a filename, row, round or event window. An event mapped to conflicting source labels requires resolution rather than an invented split or silent repair.
2. **Group same-date events.** All events in a tour sharing a source date form one simultaneous batch. Neither event name, surface, round, match number, filename nor row order breaks a tie. Cross-tour batches remain independent even when labels match.
3. **Freeze before the target batch.** Every target match in a batch uses the same pre-batch information snapshot, with player-specific features derived only from eligible records in batches with a **strictly earlier source date**. Equal-date and later-date records cannot contribute. Every later round in an event keeps the same cutoff as its earlier rounds.
4. **No within-event updates.** Apply no within-event rating or factor-history updates and no updates between events sharing a date. Stage eligible results until the entire batch is complete in the computation, then update for subsequent batches with all same-date events processed together. Computational completion means accounting for every eligible frozen row, not asserting an actual finish time or release date.
5. **Order-independent batch updates.** Future history accumulation must be invariant to row/event permutation within a batch. Any later Elo implementation must specify a synchronous update using frozen pre-batch states, without reconstructing a sequential trajectory inside the completed batch. The aggregation formula, initialization and rating parameters are not selected here; disclose and approve them before that implementation. Avoid both mid-batch state mutation and hidden sequential updates after the batch that make the next batch depend on an arbitrary ordering.
6. **Identical information cutoffs.** Four Factors and surface Elo must use identical eligible earlier-batch information and the same target-cohort comparison rules. Apply the common cutoff to opponent information, preprocessing and any other fitted state. Differences in feature computability must be reported and reconciled in a common evaluable cohort; they cannot give one method later information.
7. **Preserve exclusions.** Exclude retirements and their partial statistics, walkovers, quarantined records and all other frozen exclusions from updates. Preserve their existing audit records; no broader status inference or recovery is allowed. Retain the existing seven approved Montreal recovery bundles separately as already admitted. Undefined opportunities and missing histories remain explicit, never zero-filled or silently imputed.
8. **No calendar-time features.** Do not calculate elapsed-time features, inactivity adjustments or time decay from source labels or batch gaps. A label comparison supplies ordinal batching only. Unobserved matches, missing seasons and omitted events remain absent; a gap supplies no evidence of inactivity.
9. **Training-fold isolation.** Fit preprocessing and later model choices inside chronological training folds only, ordered by source label for this sensitivity. Do not split a simultaneous batch across training and held-out rows. Learn scaling, transformations and any later selection/tuning only within permitted training folds; later labels and held-out outcomes cannot influence earlier fitted state. No folds, history window, imputation procedure, hyperparameter or model are fitted/selected in this phase.
10. **Mandatory result label.** Label every later table, artifact and claim from this lane as a **source-label development sensitivity** under `SOURCE_LABEL_EVENT_BATCHING_FOR_DEVELOPMENT_SENSITIVITY`. Preserve the distinction from verified historical or deployable forecasts in metadata and prose.

## Assumptions and known limits

The convention assumes that eligible information assigned to an earlier source-date batch can be used at a later source-date batch. That is a computational assumption, not evidence that the earlier matches actually finished or their results/statistics were available before later play. Distinct-label events can overlap; suspensions or late finishes can cross label boundaries. Equal-label grouping prevents one form of arbitrary tie ordering but does not solve overlap between different labels. No fixed lag, timezone or fabricated timestamp is introduced to disguise these limits.

Later archive captures may contain corrections or information published after the assumed cutoff. Saved retrieval timestamps, complete score syntax and printed release text do not authenticate historical availability. Freezing before a label batch prevents current-batch data from entering its features under the convention; it does not demonstrate freedom from real historical leakage.

The cohort is conditional on source presence, normal completion, valid counts and frozen exclusions. Those retrospective eligibility conditions may themselves be unknown before actual play. This sensitivity does not model retirement risk or establish complete-event coverage. Some targets may have no eligible earlier-batch history; they must remain accounted for with an explicit unsupported/cold-start disposition. No missing record is manufactured and no subset is described as the unchanged full cohort.

The same cutoff for Four Factors and Elo is necessary for a fair sensitivity comparison but does not prove equal scientific usefulness, historical availability or valid uncertainty. S08's outcome coupling and the existing uncertainty limitations remain. Verified chronology gates still govern any claim to actual historical forecast readiness or deployment.
